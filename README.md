# The Top 3 Highest-Return Active Trading Strategies — and How to Actually Learn Them

A research-grounded, honest curriculum on the three active trading strategies with the
strongest evidence for high **risk-adjusted** returns that a dedicated individual can
realistically capture — not the ones with the flashiest marketing.

> **Read this first — the one-paragraph reality check.** "Highest return" is inseparable
> from "highest risk." There is no high-return, low-risk corner of markets. The base rates
> for active trading are brutal and consistent across every market and decade studied: most
> active retail traders underperform a simple index fund or lose money outright, and even
> ~94% of professional active US equity funds trailed the index over 20 years (SPIVA). This
> guide is **education, not financial advice.** It teaches strategies with genuine, published
> edges — but every one of them has multi-year losing stretches, and the standard path to
> ruin is leverage plus a bad day. For most people, a low-cost broad-market index fund held
> for decades is the evidence-based default, and these strategies are best run as small,
> systematic *satellite* sleeves around that core, sized so the worst realistic case is
> survivable.

## What "active trading strategy" means here — and how the top 3 were chosen

*Active* means you make ongoing buy/sell decisions to try to beat (or diversify) a
buy-and-hold index — as opposed to *passive* indexing. There are dozens of active styles
(day trading, scalping, swing trading, breakouts, mean-reversion, news trading, options
directional bets, and more). To pick a defensible **top 3**, we didn't ask "which has the
biggest backtest number" (that's how people get scammed). We ranked on three criteria:

1. **Documented risk-adjusted return** — real Sharpe ratios and returns, not raw CAGR.
2. **Robustness of the evidence** — does the edge show up *out-of-sample*, across *many
   markets*, across *many decades*, with *live-fund* corroboration — or is it a curve-fit
   backtest / survivorship illusion?
3. **Capturability by an individual** — can a dedicated person actually *capture* the edge
   with realistic capital and tools, or only admire it in a paper that assumes free shorting
   and zero costs?

On those criteria, three strategies separate cleanly from the pack. All three are
momentum/volatility-premium families with deep academic **and** live-fund support. We
deliberately excluded discretionary day trading and scalping (negative expected value for
retail — roughly 80–97% of persistent day traders lose money), and demoted pairs/stat-arb
and short-term reversal (a real gross edge that is largely a liquidity-provision premium
eaten by costs, crowded, and mostly institutional).

## The Top 3 at a glance

| # | Strategy | What you do | Time horizon | Honest realistic return | Defining risk |
|---|----------|-------------|--------------|------------------------|---------------|
| **1** | **[Trend Following / Managed Futures](strategies/01-trend-following.md)** | Systematically go long rising markets and short falling ones across a diversified basket of futures (stocks, bonds, FX, commodities) | Weeks–months | Live benchmark (SG Trend Index) **~4.9%/yr net since 2000, Sharpe ~0.3–0.5** standalone. Real value is diversification + "crisis alpha." | Morale-crushing multi-year flat stretches; ~30–40% win rate; futures leverage → margin-call blowups |
| **2** | **[Cross-Sectional Momentum](strategies/02-cross-sectional-momentum.md)** | Rank a universe by trailing 3–12 month return, hold the winners, avoid the losers, rebalance monthly/quarterly | 1–6 months | Investable long-only version historically **~1%/yr over the index** (and recently near zero) — roughly *equity-like* returns with a small tilt | "Momentum crashes" (the factor lost **>70% in months of 2009**); multi-year droughts; negative skew |
| **3** | **[Volatility Risk Premium (option-premium selling)](strategies/03-volatility-risk-premium.md)** | Systematically sell index options as *defined-risk* spreads to harvest the gap between implied and realized volatility | Days–weeks per trade | Disciplined defined-risk seller: **low-to-mid single digits** with a high (~70–85%) win rate; caps upside | Negative skew — "picking up pennies in front of a steamroller"; a single event (Feb 2018 "Volmageddon") can erase years of gains |

**Ranking rationale in one paragraph:** *Trend following* ranks **#1** because it has the
single deepest out-of-sample record of any anomaly (positive in every decade of a simulated
record back to the 1880s across ~67 markets), it is faithfully investable in its *real*
long/short form via cheap managed-futures funds, it has genuine **positive skew**, and it
delivers documented *crisis alpha* — it tends to pay off precisely when a stock/bond
portfolio is bleeding. *Cross-sectional momentum* ranks **#2** as Fama's "premier anomaly"
with the highest raw in-sample Sharpe of the classic equity factors and ~200 years of
support, but is demoted because the fat part of the paper premium lives in the un-tradeable
short leg / small caps, and the accessible long-only version historically added only ~1%/yr
— *and it carries a nasty negative-skew crash risk of its own.* *Volatility Risk Premium*
ranks **#3**: the best-documented options edge, structurally grounded, retail-accessible via
defined-risk spreads — but its defining negative skew, tail risk, and capped upside keep it
below the two momentum strategies. **The honest meta-point: even the winners are modest net
(live Sharpe ~0.4–0.6), and for most people these belong as small systematic satellites
around a cheap index core.**

### The universal method that applies to all three

Whatever you trade, the winners share the same skeleton:

- **Rules in writing, decided while calm.** Universe, signal, sizing, exits, and a
  pre-committed drawdown protocol — written down *before* a dollar is at risk. Discretion is
  where edges go to die.
- **Size by risk, not by dollars, and never bet the farm on one trade.** Every strategy here
  has a position-sizing formula and a portfolio-heat cap. Higher yield is always bought with
  a fatter left tail.
- **Backtest → paper trade → tiny real → scale slowly.** And only scale *after* you've
  survived a real drawdown *by the rules*, not after a lucky winning streak.
- **Journal everything, including your emotions.** The trade log that records "I wanted to
  take profit early / skip this short / add size" is how you catch your own discipline
  slipping before it costs you.
- **Judge yourself on process and survivability, not on any single month's P&L.**
